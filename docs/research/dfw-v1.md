**Design implications (research agent):** Sales tax: treat it as a pass-through liability, not revenue. Charge a flat 8.25% at every Dallas and Fort Worth site, with a per-product taxable flag. Bread, milk, eggs, coffee, frozen pizza and generic OTC medicine are exempt. Soda, shampoo, cosmetics, apparel and electronics are taxed, and apparel under $100 is exempt on the first Fri–Sun of August. Remit monthly on the 20th (quarterly if state tax is under $1,500 a quarter); the 0.5% timely discount is income and late filing adds the $50 fee plus a 5%/10% penalty.  Franchise tax: accrue it once a year (May 15 report) using the lesser of 70% of revenue, revenue minus $1M, or revenue minus COGS/compensation, times 0.375%, with EZ at 0.331% as an alternative under $20M. Most single-store entities owe $0 because revenue is at or below $2.65M or the computed tax is under $1,000. There is no income-tax line.  Property tax: take a Jan 1 snapshot of each store's inventory and fixtures, subtract the $125,000 per-location exemption, and multiply the excess by about 2.2% (Dallas 2.227%, Fort Worth 2.163% per $100). **Design implications (research agent):** Model loss as two separate ledger streams. The first is a theft/process shrink rate on all inventory, drawn per store from the NRSS FY2022 distribution (mean 1.6%, median 1.4%, 13% of retailers at 3% or more). Raise it for the drugstore and grocery formats (above 2%), set apparel at about 1.9%, and leave electronics at the 1.6% default (unsourced). Split it 36% external theft, 29% employee theft, 27% process error and 7% unknown, and recognize it only at a periodic physical count (Yesway counts quarterly) as a book-to-physical adjustment to COGS/shrink expense. The second is perishable spoilage driven by shelf life and order size. **Design implications (research agent):** Model electricity as each format's kWh/sf/yr times a Texas commercial price, not as a flat $/sf. Use these intensities: dry retail (apparel, electronics, drugstore) about 14–17 kWh/sf (CBECS US 13.7, West South Central 16.0); convenience about 51; grocery about 56. Use a base price of about 8.6¢/kWh (EIA TX 2025 8.64, 2026 YTD 8.56) plus a small-account premium up to about 9.7–12.7¢. That gives roughly $1.2–2.0/sf/yr for dry retail and $4.4–6.4/sf/yr for convenience/grocery. A 2,500-sf in-line c-store comes out at about $900–1,350 per month, well below the NACS $5,030 average for fuel-selling stores. Natural gas should be optional (about half of buildings use it): roughly $0.22–0.26/sf for retail and $0.75–0.80/sf for food formats at $10.98/Mcf, plus a fixed Atmos customer charge of $82–137/month. Water is minor for dry retail (about $0.06/sf plus a $23–73/month meter charge) but material for food formats. Accrue usage daily, bill monthly, and keep refrigeration-heavy formats about 3.5× dry retail per sf. Fixed customer charges should set a monthly utility floor even for an idle store.

**Design implications (research agent):** Model each store's ad response as a geometric ad stock with a concave (log or diminishing-returns) sales response, sales multiplier = (1 + stock)^e. Set e for mature V1 categories low: grocery/drugstore CPG about 0.01-0.05 (Shapiro 2020 median 0.014; Lodish 0.05; Sethuraman median 0.05), apparel about 0.05-0.10, and electronics (durables) higher, about 0.10-0.20 (durable coefficient +0.29 vs nonfood). Cap long-run effects at about 2x short-run, consistent with Sethuraman (LT/ST = 2.0) and Köhler (mass-media long-term share 0.52). With a daily tick, decay the ad stock by about 0.978-0.985/day (monthly about 0.51-0.63) for radio, TV and cable, and faster for targeted direct mail/EDDM (about 0.963/day, monthly about 0.32; median 90% duration 2 months), so most of a campaign's effect is gone within 3-6 months. Make the response depend on weekly frequency with a threshold and saturation (little effect below about 1 exposure/week, gains flattening past about 3/week, mild wearout of a creative over about a 3-month half-life per Naik), and charge real per-unit costs: EDDM $0.26/piece Retail (max 5,000/day/ZIP; $0.247 before Jul 12 2026) or about $0.26-0.31 BMEU, DFW cable spots about $150…

**Design implications (research agent):** The player is a brand-new independent firm, so default rivals and the player's store should follow the firm-age hazard from Census BDS rather than BLS establishment survival, which chain openings inflate (84% at 1 year for retail). BDS exit rates are about 23-25% in year 1, ~17% in year 2, ~13% in year 3, ~11% in years 4-5, ~9% at 6-10 years and 5-7% when mature.
