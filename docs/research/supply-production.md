# Supply Chain, Commodities & Production — Research Notes

Compiled 2026-09-30 for the Ledger & Lot economic model (US, 2024–2026 data).

**Tiers:** **T1** = exact figure from an official document or dataset. **T2** = credible industry, survey or press figure (a range where sources differ). **T3** = our own estimate, with the derivation stated.

**Context to keep in mind (2026):** In 2026, a Middle East conflict pushed energy prices up sharply. Brent averaged $104/bbl in Q2 2026. The 3-2-1 crack spread and diesel set multi-decade highs. Container and air freight rates are well above their 2024–25 levels. For a "normal year" game baseline, use the 2023–2025 annual averages rather than the August 2026 spot prices.

- Grocery and food: farm inputs 15–20% of retail. Manufacturer COGS 40–55% of retail. Retailer margin 25–35%.

- Heavy and commodity industry: COGS 70–85% of selling price. Material-dominated, with labor 10–15%.

## Gaps

1. **Census ASM by industry (NAICS)**: materials, payroll and shipments by industry. This would turn "labor % factory" and "COGS %" into T1 values per industry, but the API requires a key. Get a free key at api.census.gov, then query `timeseries/asm/industry` with RCPTOT, CSTMTOT, PAYANN, VALADD.
2. **Retail gross margin by store type**: the Census Annual Retail Trade Survey (ARTS) has 2022 data and was then folded into the Annual Integrated Economic Survey (AIES). Pull these for T1 wholesale-to-retail markups.
3. **10-year ranges for HRC, lumber, eggs and crack spread** are T3, from memory. FRED series WPU101704 (HRC PPI), LBS futures history and APU0000708111 (eggs) would firm them up.
4. **Rail ¢/ton-mile** is the 2022 value (latest found). The STB Waybill sample or AAR Railroad Facts 2025 would give a current figure.
5. **Shale breakeven and iron ore C1 costs** were read from secondary summaries of the Dallas Fed and company reports. Re-read the primary documents to promote them to T1.
6. **Cigarette, beer and gasoline tax rates** are from memory. Confirm on TTB and EIA pages.
7. **Missing BOM teardowns**: TV, bicycle, shampoo, frozen pizza, canned food, paper, glass, tires and generic pills are T3. A dedicated teardown or 10-K gross-margin pass (e.g., Coca-Cola Consolidated, Hershey, Bridgestone, Teva) would improve them.
8. **Silicon wafer**: no SEMI official ASP was found. Blank-wafer prices vary 5× across sources.
9. **US farm-gate vs export prices**: the WB grain prices are Gulf export prices. US farm prices run ~$1–2/bu lower. For 2026 monthly farm prices, use the NASS Agricultural Prices tables (the PDF returned 403 to direct fetch).
10. **2026 is an energy-shock year.** The game baseline should probably use 2023–25 averages, with 2026 as a "crisis" scenario.
